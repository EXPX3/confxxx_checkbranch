\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Aerial robots need a representation of the space they can traverse and the objects that occupy it. A camera image reveals visible surfaces, but navigation, inspection, and landing decisions also depend on metric distance, free space, and partially observed structure. Semantic scene completion (SSC) addresses this gap by predicting a dense volumetric grid of empty space and semantic categories. Predictions of unobserved regions can support anticipatory planning under uncertainty and can be revised as new observations arrive. Such predictions remain learned hypotheses: a plausible completed volume does not establish that an unobserved region is safe. This distinction motivates evaluating geometric completion and semantic recognition separately.

RGB cameras are attractive for uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs) because they provide rich appearance information with modest sensor mass. Recovering three-dimensional structure from them is nevertheless difficult. A monocular image does not uniquely determine metric scale, and an aerial viewpoint changes apparent object size with flight height. Roofs, vegetation, transport surfaces, and thin infrastructure can appear under oblique or near-top-down views. Occlusion and repeated texture complicate depth recovery, while small image footprints make some categories difficult to recognize. An aerial SSC system must therefore solve two related problems: assigning observations to the correct metric locations and identifying the occupied structures represented there.

Modern visual foundation models offer transferable features without requiring task-specific backbone training. We study frozen V-JEPA~2.1 image/video representations and frozen DINOv3 image representations for monocular depth estimation (MDE) and SSC. V-JEPA~2.1 applies dense predictive supervision to visible and masked tokens and deep supervision across encoder levels; its final representation retains local information useful for dense prediction \cite[Secs.~2.3.1--2.3.2 and App.~D.1]{murlabadia2026vjepa21}. DINOv3 supplies a complementary image representation \cite{simeoni2025dinov3}. Their downstream usefulness cannot be inferred from pretraining objectives alone. Metric aerial depth requires a task decoder, and SSC additionally requires geometric lifting, volumetric reasoning, and supervision that preserves rare occupied classes.

We use OccuFly \cite{gross2026occufly}, which provides real aerial RGB observations, metric depth, calibration, poses, altitude metadata, and semantic occupancy labels. Its common scene-disjoint partition permits depth and occupancy to be studied in the same domain. Our central question is: \emph{how do frozen visual representations, metric geometry, and voxel decoding contribute to aerial scene completion, and which limitations remain after occupied-space recovery improves?} We examine this question through an evidence-driven sequence of depth probes, dense decoders, and SSC configurations. The study concerns transfer and the behavior of completed systems; it does not introduce a new foundation pretraining objective.

For depth, a last-layer linear probe tests whether useful geometry is accessible through a small head. A Dense Prediction Transformer (DPT) decoder \cite{ranftl2021dpt} adds multilevel reconstruction, while Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) \cite{perez2018film} supplies altitude and camera intrinsics to the decoder. A Simple Feature Pyramid (SFP) adapter \cite{li2022vitdet} and temporal attention provide additional design diagnostics. We distinguish these mechanisms because decoder capacity, camera metadata, and neighboring images are different sources of information. A completed pose-aware video diagnostic is reported on its own context-valid population rather than ranked as a full-test monocular result.

For SSC, we separate image context from geometry. Monocular depth supplies one geometric prior; a frozen MVSFormer++ provider \cite{cao2024mvsformerpp} supplies a calibrated multiview stereo (MVS) posterior. FoundationSSC-inspired lifting and fusion \cite{chen2025foundationssc} construct the voxel representation. VoxDet then predicts semantics together with directional semantic-boundary offsets derived by the Voxel-to-Instance transformation (VoxNT) \cite{li2025voxdet}. Our implemented treatment uses full-resolution class-balanced offset supervision and an objective that separates binary occupancy from conditional occupied-class recognition. These components have distinct roles, so a gain of the complete treatment must not be attributed to one component without a matching ablation.

Three independent runs of the final DINOv3--MVSFormer++--VoxDet treatment yield $49.33\pm1.17\%$ scene-completion intersection over union (SC IoU) and $4.80\pm0.36\%$ 21-class semantic mean IoU (mIoU). The substantial difference between these scores is informative: the model recovers occupied structure much more successfully than it distinguishes the complete occupied taxonomy. Class-wise evidence shows persistent failures for some represented categories and identifies categories with no test support. This analysis prevents aggregate completion gains from being interpreted as uniform semantic competence.

The contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
\item A frozen-representation aerial depth study that separates linear probing, dense decoding, camera conditioning, and a context-valid temporal/multiview diagnostic, with explicit result provenance.
\item A depth-to-SSC comparison combining monocular or frozen calibrated multiview geometry with instance-aware voxel decoding, followed by three-run evaluation of the final treatment.
\item A protocol-aware semantic analysis that defines metric denominators, distinguishes absent categories from observed failures, and relates audited test support to per-class behavior without inferring a frequency-only cause.
\end{itemize}

Throughout, repeated-run variability is reported only where repetitions exist; single-run architectural comparisons remain descriptive.
