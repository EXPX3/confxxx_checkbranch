\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Aerial robots require metric geometry and semantic occupancy beyond the surfaces directly visible in an RGB image. Monocular depth estimation (MDE) locates visible surfaces, whereas semantic scene completion (SSC) predicts occupied and empty voxels, including occluded regions. The latter remains a learned hypothesis rather than a safety guarantee. Both tasks are difficult from aerial viewpoints: metric scale changes with altitude, oblique views and repeated texture complicate correspondence, and small or thin objects occupy few pixels and voxels.

We study whether frozen V-JEPA~2.1 image/video and DINOv3 image features transfer to aerial MDE and SSC \cite{murlabadia2026vjepa21,simeoni2025dinov3}. OccuFly provides aligned RGB, metric depth, calibration, poses, altitude, and semantic occupancy under a scene-disjoint split \cite{gross2026occufly}. We compare linear and DPT depth heads \cite{ranftl2021dpt}, camera-conditioned FiLM \cite{perez2018film}, temporal alignment, and calibrated multiview geometry. For SSC, monocular or MVSFormer++ depth \cite{cao2024mvsformerpp} drives FoundationSSC-style lifting \cite{chen2025foundationssc}, followed by either the retained voxel head or VoxDet decoding \cite{li2025voxdet}. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes this context--geometry--voxel pipeline; Figs.~\ref{fig:backbone_feature_diagnostics} and~\ref{fig:dta_raft_compact} visualize the frozen features and temporal alignment used by its depth branch.

The experiments ask whether frozen features expose metric geometry, whether altitude and intrinsics improve dense readout, whether motion-aware temporal or calibrated multiview evidence improves geometry, and whether SSC is limited primarily by depth, lifting, or semantic recognition. The strongest completed MDE configuration, DINOv3 FiLM-DPT, reaches 4.362~m RMSE. The final DINOv3--MVSFormer++--VoxDet treatment reaches $49.33\pm1.17\%$ scene-completion IoU and $4.80\pm0.36\%$ semantic mIoU across three runs. The gap shows that occupied-space recovery improves more than fine-grained recognition.

Our contribution is a controlled frozen-feature depth comparison, a depth-to-SSC evaluation spanning monocular and calibrated multiview geometry, and an audited class-wise analysis that distinguishes unsupported classes from observed failures. Repeated-run statistics are reported only where independent runs exist; other architectural comparisons remain descriptive.
