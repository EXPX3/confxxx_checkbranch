\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Aerial Geometry and Task-Aligned Supervision}
The information required to evaluate a three-dimensional perception system depends on the output being predicted. A two-dimensional segmentation dataset can measure recognition of visible pixels, but it cannot directly evaluate the semantic content of an occluded voxel. Similarly, depth maps assess visible surface geometry without supplying a dense labeled free-space volume. UAVid provides aerial semantic segmentation \cite{lyu2020uavid}, and Mid-Air offers synthetic multimodal drone observations \cite{fonder2019midair}. These resources address important aerial perception questions, but their supervision is not interchangeable with SSC labels. OccuFly provides an aerial benchmark with joint depth and semantic occupancy supervision \cite{gross2026occufly}; we use its standard scene-disjoint partition rather than combining scores from different data populations.

\subsection{Frozen Representations and Dense Depth Decoding}
V-JEPA~2.1 and DINOv3 differ in their pretraining and modality, but both expose spatial feature maps suitable for frozen-feature transfer \cite{murlabadia2026vjepa21,simeoni2025dinov3}. Freezing the encoder makes it possible to compare task heads while retaining a fixed representation within each experiment. It does not make two backbone families identical: channel dimensions, model capacity, and pretraining data remain different. We consequently interpret a cross-backbone result as the performance of a representation--head configuration rather than a causal estimate of the pretraining objective.

DPT reassembles transformer features and combines them through dense refinement \cite{ranftl2021dpt}. The SFP construction used with plain vision transformers provides another route to multiscale decoding from a single map \cite{li2022vitdet}. These alternatives separate access to several encoder levels from spatial rescaling of one final level. FiLM conditions intermediate activations through channel-wise affine modulation \cite{perez2018film}. In our setting, the conditioning vector contains altitude and normalized intrinsics, allowing a task head to adapt its interpretation of frozen appearance features. Comparisons across these heads are useful diagnostics of accessible geometric information, but absent repeated, equal-budget controls they do not establish that one individual mechanism causes every observed difference.

Temporal aggregation at a fixed image coordinate does not compensate for camera or object motion. RAFT estimates dense optical flow through recurrent updates over all-pairs feature correlations \cite{teed2020raft}. Forward--backward flow agreement can additionally identify unreliable or occluded correspondences \cite{meister2018unflow}. We use these ideas only in an alignment diagnostic: frozen RAFT flow drives a deterministic bilinear resampling of contextual V-JEPA tubelet features before temporal readout. This is distinct from learning optical flow and from calibrated MVS, which uses camera pose and metric depth hypotheses.

\subsection{Calibrated Multiview Geometry}
MVS estimates depth by finding evidence for a target surface across calibrated views. For a candidate depth, target and source pixels are related by projective geometry, so feature agreement can be evaluated after camera motion is compensated. MVSFormer++ develops a transformer-based MVS architecture \cite{cao2024mvsformerpp}. We use a locally trained native provider with frozen weights during SSC and adapt its categorical depth posterior to the lifting grid. This distinguishes multiview geometry from temporal pooling: neighboring frames alone do not impose correspondence, whereas pose-aware projection explicitly relates them.

The distinction also matters for evaluation. A temporal method may exclude sequence-boundary targets that lack context, and an MVS dataset join may retain only references with valid calibration and source views. These populations must be stated before their scores are compared. Furthermore, an improved dense depth expectation need not imply improved lifting. Two posteriors can have similar expectations while assigning different probability mass to the voxel depths where image features are accumulated. Our study therefore follows the geometry representation into SSC rather than treating a depth leaderboard as a complete explanation of occupancy behavior.

\subsection{Completion, Context, and Directional Structure}
SSCNet established joint volumetric completion and semantics from depth \cite{song2017sscnet}, while MonoScene extends completion to monocular RGB \cite{cao2022monoscene}. VoxFormer uses depth-guided sparse voxel reasoning \cite{li2023voxformer}; CGFormer develops context- and geometry-aware voxel transformation \cite{yu2024cgformer}. Symphonies and DISC use instance/context reasoning to improve scene completion \cite{jiang2024symphonies,liu2025disc}. These methods show that lifting visible evidence and reasoning about unobserved structure are linked but separable design problems.

FoundationSSC combines semantic and geometric priors, while VoxDet changes the downstream voxel decoder by separating semantic prediction from directional offset regression \cite{chen2025foundationssc,li2025voxdet}. VoxNT derives the offset targets deterministically from semantic labels. It is not an additional pretrained network, and it does not recover true instance identity from touching objects with the same semantic class. Our hybrid combines a foundation-based frontend with VoxDet-style decoding and local loss adaptations. We distinguish this implementation from the original published architectures and avoid transferring their efficiency claims to our aerial configuration.
