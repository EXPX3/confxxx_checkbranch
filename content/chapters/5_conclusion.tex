\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support configuration-level conclusions. Dense, camera-conditioned heads recover more metric depth than the corresponding linear probes, and the final frozen DINOv3--MVSFormer++ frontend with VoxDet gives a repeatable occupied-space improvement. However, the SSC variants also differ in representation, geometry, decoder, objective, and optimization. Without a matched factorial study, the gain cannot be assigned to MVS, VoxNT, or one loss term alone.

Depth accuracy and SSC need not rank identically: lifting depends on posterior mass along each ray, and boundary errors can move evidence between voxels. O5 confirms geometric headroom, but it is a privileged single-run diagnostic, not a calibrated obstacle-risk posterior or an upper bound. Its residual error motivates better depth, context/fusion, voxel decoding, and long-tail data rather than depth alone.

FoundationSSC's supplementary failure analysis reports merged voxels between partially occluded cars when instance supervision is absent \cite{chen2025foundationssc}. Our hybrid retains VoxDet's spatially decoupled encoder before the semantic and regression heads, and its predicted offsets guide semantic aggregation. Nevertheless, VoxNT derives boundaries from semantic runs rather than true instance IDs, so touching same-class objects can still merge. Together with zero true positives for represented person, bicycle, and cable classes, this shows why stronger completion does not imply reliable recognition.

The evidence is limited to two OccuFly test scenes and three represented altitudes; nearby frames are correlated. It does not establish transfer across cameras, environments, weather, or online pose error. The five-view model also requires calibration and overlap. Finally, frozen encoders reduce adaptation, not inference cost: latency, memory, and energy were not measured on embedded hardware. Deployment claims therefore require matched controls, per-target confusion analysis, external datasets, pose-robustness tests, and complete-system profiling.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Frozen image/video representations support aerial metric depth when paired with suitable dense heads. Combining frozen DINOv3 context, calibrated MVSFormer++ geometry, and VoxDet decoding improves repeatable scene completion on OccuFly, while class-wise results expose substantial unresolved semantic errors. The central finding is therefore a separation: stronger geometry improves occupied-space recovery, but robust long-tail aerial recognition remains open.
