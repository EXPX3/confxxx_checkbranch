\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion_conclusion}

\paragraph{What is linearly accessible?}
The frozen V-JEPA~2.1 last-layer probe reaches 7.795~m RMSE and 0.225 SILog on the standard test split. The corrected four-level DPT improves these to 6.730~m and 0.200, respectively. Thus the data do not support the earlier conclusion that a linear probe outperforms dense decoding. Instead, aerial geometry is present but benefits from recovering and fusing intermediate spatial structure. The matched DINOv3 linear probe will determine whether this accessibility is specific to V-JEPA or shared by large frozen ViTs.

\paragraph{What does FiLM establish?}
The strongest traceable V-JEPA~2.1 FiLM-DPT reaches 4.770~m RMSE, 0.131 AbsRel, and 0.166 SILog. Relative to the corrected plain DPT, this is consistent with altitude/intrinsics helping metric scale recovery. However, the available FiLM result comes from a sweep rather than a fully matched multi-seed pair, so the current evidence supports association, not a precise causal effect size. We no longer compare FiLM against the failed 11.471~m historical DPT checkpoint. DepthAnything2 remains stronger in SILog and threshold accuracy even when V-JEPA FiLM-DPT has slightly lower RMSE, indicating a tradeoff between metric error and scale-invariant consistency.

\paragraph{Where do video and SSC help?}
Temporal aggregation is altitude- and scene-dependent and does not consistently beat RGB: it helps at 30~m and on scene 08, but loses at 40/50~m and on scene 09. Since OccuFly exposes short sampled neighborhoods rather than continuous video, this negative result does not rule out benefits from dense streams, motion history, or dynamic cues. Pose-aware warping is the appropriate control for camera motion and is evaluated directly in depth space. For SSC, the FiLM-DPT VoxDet V5-V2 recipe with full-resolution VoxNT, long-tail loss, and scheduled depth gradients reaches 45.77/4.62 SC IoU/SSC mIoU. Applying its compatible decoder, objective, and full-resolution VoxNT components to frozen DINOv3 MVSFormer++ geometry reaches $49.33\pm1.17$ SC IoU and $5.22\pm0.35$ union-present mIoU across three seeds ($5.60\pm0.43$ GT-present; $4.80\pm0.36$ fixed-21). Every seed exceeds the single historical MVS control in the directly comparable fields, while the unrepeated control prevents a paired significance claim. The MVS posterior remains frozen, so scheduled depth gradients are not active in this treatment. UAV11 remapping reaches 9.32 validation mIoU versus 5.64 for the original fine taxonomy, showing that sparse and visually overlapping labels materially depress macro IoU; the changed label space makes this a diagnostic rather than a direct benchmark gain.

\paragraph{Limitations and conclusion.}
This is a frozen-feature study on one primary joint MDE/SSC dataset. It does not establish onboard efficiency, end-to-end finetuning behavior, cross-dataset real-flight transfer, statistical significance, or direct Pose-MVS depth quality. The altitude interpolation diagnostic reuses scenes across elevations and is therefore not causal evidence for conditioning. Within these limits, the corrected results show that large frozen self-supervised ViTs contain useful aerial geometry; decoder structure and calibrated metadata determine how effectively it becomes metric depth, while downstream SSC gains depend more strongly on geometry--context alignment than on swapping one depth prior.
