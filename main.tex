\begin{abstract}
Aerial 3D perception must recover metric geometry and semantic occupancy despite altitude-dependent scale, oblique viewpoints, and limited onboard sensing. We study what frozen image and video foundation representations transfer to monocular depth estimation (MDE) and semantic scene completion (SSC) on OccuFly. Under a common $384\!\times\!384$ protocol, we progress from last-layer linear probes to four-level DPT decoding, altitude/intrinsics-conditioned FiLM-DPT, dense temporal aggregation, and pose-aware multiview lifting. We additionally introduce matched frozen DINOv3 controls to separate conclusions about V-JEPA~2.1 from properties shared by large self-supervised ViTs. Correcting an anomalous historical DPT baseline changes the central interpretation: multi-level decoding improves over linear probing, while FiLM must be assessed against a healthy, capacity-matched DPT rather than against a failed run. The matched DINOv3 FiLM-DPT reaches 4.358~m test RMSE, compared with 4.770~m for V-JEPA~2.1 FiLM-DPT, 6.730~m for the corrected V-JEPA plain DPT, and 7.795~m for its linear probe. SSC experiments show that geometry, lifting, and semantic context must remain aligned: combining DINOv3 MVSFormer++ geometry with full-resolution VoxNT and long-tail optimization reaches 48.06 SC IoU and 4.84 union-present SSC mIoU. A UAV-oriented 11-class remapping reaches 9.32 validation mIoU, indicating that sparse, visually overlapping fine classes explain part---but not all---of the low semantic score. Temporal aggregation is inconsistent on snapshot neighborhoods; its value may require continuous video with explicit correspondence. Our study therefore characterizes where frozen representations expose aerial geometry, where decoder structure and camera metadata matter, and which apparent gains do not survive controlled comparison.
\end{abstract}
