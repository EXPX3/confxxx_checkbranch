\begin{abstract}
Aerial 3D scene understanding is essential for autonomous UAV operations such as cargo delivery, search-and-rescue, and human-UAV collaboration. Camera-based perception is attractive for these settings given its light weight, low cost, availability, and compatibility with UAV size, weight, and power constraints. This motivates methods that can infer geometry, semantics, and free space from visual observations, making Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) and Semantic Scene Completion (SSC) key components of UAV 3D perception that enable 3D scene understanding. Aerial imagery introduces altitude-dependent scale variation, oblique viewpoints, weak texture, and different occlusion patterns, demanding domain-specific solutions. Using OccuFly, a real-world aerial dataset with RGB images, metric depth maps, and semantic occupancy grids, we study whether V-JEPA 2.1 foundation features transfer to aerial MDE and SSC. Our ablations progress from V-JEPA 2.1 image-spatial \& video-spatiotemporal feature handling, from linear probing to DPT (Dense Prediction Transformer)-style multiscale decoding, FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation)-based altitude \& intrinsics conditioning for MDE, followed by injecting conditioned depth-estimation priors, deploying a learnable UAV pose-aware multi-view stereo depth-estimation module, and transferring spatiotemporal features for SSC. Through this staged analysis, we separate the contributions of representation transfer, altitude conditioning, and depth-estimation variants to improving aerial SSC.

\end{abstract}
